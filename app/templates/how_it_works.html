How SignalCare AI works

SignalCare AI surfaces signals for a counselor to review — it is decision support, not a diagnosis or crisis verdict. Every signal below is a pattern flag with a confidence score, intended to help a human reviewer notice things faster, not to replace their judgment.

Pipeline overview

When a session recording is analyzed, each patient turn moves through the same pipeline, in order:

  1. Transcription. The recording is transcribed into timestamped segments (patient/therapist turns).
  2. PII redaction. Names, locations, and other identifying details are redacted from the transcript text before any further processing.
  3. Sentiment scoring. Each patient turn is classified as positive, negative, or neutral, with a confidence score.
  4. Utterance-level distress signals (S01–S04, S06, S07, S10, S11, S13–S18). Each patient turn is checked against the distress signal taxonomy below, returning any signals present with a confidence score.
  5. Temporal & session-level signals (S05, S08, S09, S12). A rolling window over recent turns recomputes emotional volatility, sentiment drift, rumination, and the escalation composite after every new turn. See "How the temporal signals work" below.

On the session dashboard, this all happens turn-by-turn as the recording plays: the transcript line, sentiment, and any fired signals for that turn appear together, and the session-level badges (S05/S08/S09/S12) update live.

How the temporal signals work

S05, S08, S09, and S12 are not judged from a single utterance — they look at a rolling window of the most recent 5 patient turns and recompute as each new turn arrives. This mirrors how a counselor would notice a pattern: not from one sentence, but from how the last few minutes of conversation have gone.

S08 · Negative sentiment trend (drift)

Each turn's sentiment (positive / neutral / negative) is converted to a score from -1 to +1, weighted by the model's confidence. Over the rolling window, a linear regression line is fit to these scores. If the slope of that line is steeper than -0.15 — i.e. sentiment is consistently getting worse turn over turn, not just dipping once — S08 fires.

S08 is suppressed when S05 (volatility) also fires in the same window, since a single sharp emotional swing can otherwise produce a misleadingly negative slope that isn't really a sustained "trend."

S05 · Emotional volatility

For each pair of consecutive turns in the rolling window, the change in sentiment score is computed. S05 fires when the standard deviation of these turn-to-turn changes is ≥ 0.9 — meaning the patient's emotional tone is swinging sharply back and forth, rather than moving smoothly in one direction.

This threshold was tuned against real session data so that S05 flags roughly the most volatile fifth of turns, rather than firing on routine ups and downs.

S09 · Rumination patterns

Every patient turn is checked against the distress signal taxonomy (S01–S18). S09 fires when the same distress signal code recurs in at least 3 of the 5 turns in the rolling window — the patient is repeatedly circling back to the same kind of distressing thought, rather than mentioning it once and moving on.

S12 · Escalation risk composite

S12 is a meta-signal: it never fires from a single signal alone. Within the rolling window, all distinct distress signals with High or Medium severity are collected (including S05/S08 themselves, if they are firing on the current turn). If 4 or more distinct such signals co-occur within the window, S12 fires as a flag that overall session risk has become elevated and may warrant closer review.

S12 is intentionally rare — it is meant to highlight sessions where several different concerning patterns are converging, not to fire routinely.
